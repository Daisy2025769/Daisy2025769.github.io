HICC PET should not be analysed as a conventional pet shop. Public information positions it as a U.S.-founded pet wellness and grooming brand with DTC e-commerce, a Singapore APAC Brand Experience Center, premium grooming, community events and showroom rental. Its membership programme therefore sits at the intersection of **retail, services, CRM and experiential/community marketing** — and that intersection is the subject of this case study.

*Source: HICC SG About Us; HICC Membership Agreement; HICC LinkedIn / official company pages.*

## 1. Industry Definition & Value Chain

HICC operates across several connected layers of the pet-care economy:

| Layer | What it includes |
|---|---|
| Pet products / specialty retail | Hygiene, grooming, oral/ear/eye/skin care, accessories, food and other pet supplies |
| DTC e-commerce | Brand-owned Shopify / online store, direct fulfilment, CRM and first-party data |
| Pet services | Grooming, consultations, care kits and related services |
| Experiential retail | Showroom, product trials, consultations, workshops and events |
| Membership / CRM | Member identity, segmentation, lifecycle campaigns, referrals and retention |

**Simplified value chain:**
Manufacturing / R&D → Brand → Distribution / DTC → Retail & Showroom → Pet Owner → Services → Repeat Purchase → Customer Data → CRM / Personalisation → Higher Customer Lifetime Value (CLV).

## 2. Global Pet-Care Market

Euromonitor reports that global pet care reached approximately **US$207bn in 2025** and **US$224.5bn in 2026**. The key strategic shift is from pure volume growth toward value growth, driven by premiumisation, pet health/wellness and changing pet demographics. Euromonitor also reported cat food as the fastest-growing category from 2020–2025, at about **6% CAGR**, versus **3.8%** for dog food.

| Global signal | What the data says | Implication for HICC |
|---|---|---|
| Market scale | US$207bn (2025); US$224.5bn (2026) | Large and resilient category, but competition is increasingly selective. |
| Premiumisation | Premium segments remain important, although consumers are more price-sensitive. | HICC needs to communicate functional value, not rely only on discounts. |
| Channel evolution | E-commerce plus showroom ecosystems are reshaping pet retail. | **HICC can combine DTC data with showroom/community experience.** |

*Source: Euromonitor, The World Market for Pet Care (2025/2026) and Pet Care Industry Overview.*

## 3. Singapore Market

Singapore is attractive because spending is increasingly quality- and wellness-led, even though pet-population growth is moderate. CNA, citing Euromonitor, reported about **114,000 pet dogs** and **94,000 pet cats** in 2023. The dog population was up nearly 3% from 2019, while the cat population was up about 10%.

| Singapore signal | Validated data | Implication for HICC |
|---|---|---|
| Pet population | ~114k dogs; ~94k cats in 2023 | — |
| Humanisation | CNA reports owners increasingly treat pets as family and spend more on health/wellbeing. | Supports emotional storytelling and wellness positioning. |
| Market direction | Euromonitor 2026 highlights premiumisation, health-focused innovation, convenience and e-commerce. | Supports HICC's premium wellness + DTC model. |
| Competition | Euromonitor says the pet-products market is fragmented; the top 3 companies held <25% in 2025, with Kit Cat at 12%. | **There is room for specialised brands, but scale and distribution matter.** |

## 7. HICC Membership: Current Structure

| Current feature | Validated public terms | Case implication |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | Member registration creates a HICC Pet Club membership card automatically; no membership fee. | Low-friction acquisition is possible. |
| Benefits | Discounted products, grooming packages, events and showroom rental are listed. | Membership spans product + service + experience, which is strategically valuable. |
| Prepaid top-up | S$1,000 or S$2,000 top-up options. | High commitment may suit high-value customers but can create acquisition friction for new/low-frequency users. |
| Data | Membership agreement covers personal-data collection/use under PDPA and marketing consent/opt-out rules. | Data quality and consent architecture are foundational to CRM. |
| Prepaid services | Prepaid grooming packages are offered at a special discount and are non-refundable under the agreement, subject to stated terms. | Useful retention mechanism, but requires careful customer-fit and breakage monitoring. |

*Source: HICC Pet Club Membership Agreement, effective/current public page.*

## 10. HICC Strategic Advantage — What Is Actually Defensible

- **Product specialisation** — HICC is concentrated around daily wellness/hygiene problems rather than trying to be a one-stop pet supermarket.
- **Care kits** — the current catalogue already bundles products into problem/solution packages, creating a natural route to higher AOV.
- **Science/quality narrative** — HICC states FDA-registered manufacturing and ISO 9001/CGMP standards, and its global site highlights proprietary technologies such as HOCl and CocoPlex.
- **Experience centre** — the Singapore APAC hub at Marina One combines retail, wellness, grooming, free trials, consultations and community gatherings.
- **International channel momentum** — HICC reports expansion into thousands of stores globally and 1,380+ Petco stores in the U.S.; useful brand credibility, but should be presented as company-reported.

*Source: HICC official website, SG About Us and LinkedIn/company updates.*

## 11. Main Challenges — Distinguishing Fact from Hypothesis

| Issue | Evidence status | Why it's a risk |
|---|---|---|
| APAC/Singapore brand awareness | Strategic hypothesis; HICC is still in expansion mode and launched the Singapore APAC center in 2026. | **Requires efficient acquisition and partnerships, not only retention.** |
| Showroom geographic limitation | Business-model hypothesis, not a measured HICC fact. | Grooming is inherently location-sensitive; customer willingness to travel should be tested. |
| Small assortment vs. PLC | Validated: 29 HICC SG online items vs. PLC's claimed 15,000+ products. | HICC cannot win on breadth; it needs a sharper problem/solution proposition. |
| Membership value proposition may be unclear | The current public agreement lists benefits across products, grooming, events and showroom rental, but does not show a simple points/tier-based lifecycle. Validated current S$1k/S$2k top-up option. | Members may join without understanding the next action or the incremental value of staying engaged. Potentially powerful for cash flow/retention, but may be a barrier for first-time customers. |
| Showroom economics | Venue rental is publicly monetised, but internal utilisation is unknown. | Measure footfall, grooming occupancy, event conversion and revenue per opening hour. |

## 12. Marketing's Next Step: From Membership Card to Customer Lifecycle

The central thesis: **membership is not just a discount mechanism — it is the identity layer that connects Shopify, showroom, grooming and community.**

| Lifecycle stage | Data to capture | CRM action | Business KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acquire | Source, pet type, first product/service | Welcome + pet-profile completion | CAC, profile completion |
| Activate | First purchase, consultation, event attendance | Product education + first benefit | Activation rate |
| Second purchase | SKU, category, time since purchase | Cross-sell / care-kit recommendation | 2nd purchase rate |
| Retain | Purchase frequency, grooming history | Replenishment + grooming reminders | 90/180-day retention |
| At risk | Days since last purchase | Win-back campaign | Reactivation rate |
| Advocate | Referral, UGC, event participation | Referral reward + member story | Referral / UGC rate |
| VIP | CLV, frequency, grooming spend | Priority booking / exclusive events | CLV, member revenue share |

The **cat segment** deserves a dedicated test rather than being treated as a minor extension of the dog business. CNA's Euromonitor-cited data showed the Singapore cat population at ~94,000 in 2023, up about 10% from 2019, versus ~3% growth for dogs. Euromonitor's 2026 Singapore report also identifies the lifting of cat-ownership restrictions as a demand driver, particularly for cat food.

## 13. KPI Model Simulation — Illustrative Membership Economics

The following model uses the assumptions in the accompanying Excel case file. **All figures are illustrative targets, not HICC actuals.** The purpose is to demonstrate how the marketing team can translate CRM improvements into financial outcomes.

| Input | Illustrative value | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Members at start | 1,000 | members |
| New members / month | 120 | members |
| Member activation rate | 65% | of new members |
| Monthly member purchasers | 55% | of members |
| Average member AOV | S$85 | per order |
| Non-member AOV | S$65 | per order |
| Member purchase frequency | 1.6 | orders / month |
| Non-member purchase frequency | 1.0 | orders / month |
| Monthly member retention | 92% | of active members |
| Referral rate | 8% | of members |

| Output | Calculation formula | Illustrative result |
|---|---|---|
| Activated members | 1,000 × 65% | 650 |
| Monthly member purchasers | 1,000 × 55% | 550 |
| Member revenue / month | 550 × S$85 × 1.6 | S$74,800 |
| Non-member revenue benchmark | (1,000 − 550) × S$65 × 1.0 | S$29,250 |
| Member AOV uplift | (S$85 ÷ S$65) − 1 | 30.8% |
| Referral-generated member equivalents | 1,000 × 8% | 80 |
| Illustrative member revenue share | S$74,800 ÷ (S$74,800 + S$29,250) | 71.9% |
| Revenue uplift vs. non-member behaviour | 550 × [(S$85 × 1.6) − (S$65 × 1.0)] | S$39,050 / month |
| Annualised revenue uplift | S$39,050 × 12 | S$468,600 / year |

> This is a scenario comparison, not causal evidence. It assumes the 550 member purchasers would otherwise behave like non-members. In a real case, use cohort or treatment/control analysis to estimate incremental lift.

This model changes the discussion from "membership should improve retention" to **"which lever creates the largest incremental contribution?"**

**Suggested next tests:** (1) increase second-purchase conversion; (2) increase Care Kit penetration; (3) increase grooming repurchase; (4) test referral acquisition; (5) compare member and non-member cohorts while controlling for customer quality.

## 14. Case-Study Data That Is Still Missing

Before making a final membership recommendation, request or estimate the following — and label any estimate explicitly as "illustrative":

| Data needed | Why |
|---|---|
| Member count and active-member definition | Establish true membership penetration. |
| Customer-level order history | Calculate AOV, frequency and retention. |
| Pet type / age / breed / care needs | Enable meaningful segmentation. |
| Acquisition source | Compare CAC and quality by channel. |
| Showroom footfall and conversion | Quantify the offline funnel. |
| Grooming bookings, repeat interval and utilisation | Build service economics. |
| Gross margin by SKU / kit | Avoid recommending discounts that destroy contribution. |
| CRM/EDM open, click and conversion rates | Baseline lifecycle performance. |
| Referral / UGC activity | Measure advocacy rather than treating it as vanity engagement. |

## 15. Conclusion

HICC should not compete with PLC on assortment or scale. Its opportunity is to win on **specialised pet wellness, grooming, experience and community** — then use membership and CRM to turn those touchpoints into higher AOV, repeat purchase, retention and CLV.

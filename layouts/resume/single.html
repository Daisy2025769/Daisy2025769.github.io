Research suggests that the human brain is not built with one universal set of strengths. Different regions tend to specialize in different ways — the left hemisphere is more associated with language, logic, analytical thinking, and sequential processing, while the right hemisphere is more associated with visual-spatial thinking, holistic processing, imagination, and pattern recognition. Some strengths come naturally, some are shaped through experience and practice, and others are still waiting to grow.

Market Analysis

During my first semester at SMU, I worked on an equity research project on Hershey. I was responsible for researching the confectionery industry, with a focus on the U.S. market, and analyzing Hershey’s financial performance over the past 10 years against major competitors such as Mars. These insights were then used to support our assumptions and valuation in a DCF model.

Problem Segmentation

I believe every complex task can be broken down into smaller workflow buckets and solved step by step. That is how I naturally make sense of problems.

During my internship at Axiom, I supported the finance team with the FY2024 valuation exercise. Once I understood the expected outcome, I segmented the task into clear steps and set deadlines for each: first, building PivotTables for each fund to organize the required data; second, applying 80/20 prioritization to identify material positions; and third, reviewing quarterly reports to identify key drivers and substantiate period-over-period valuation movements.

Attention to Detail & Simplification

Studying business has taught me that details and timing matter. Whether I am working with sector data, company performance, or financial charts, I try to understand what is actually driving each rise or decline rather than simply report the numbers. At the same time, I believe good analysis should make complexity easier to understand. The final explanation should be clear, concise, and supported by evidence. In the workplace, this becomes even more important, as accuracy and clarity directly affect how efficiently people collaborate and make decisions.

Creative Marketing Strategy

From 2022 to 2023, while I was still at university, I co-ran a café with two partners and took on the role of marketing manager for 1.5 years. It was my own business, so I had the freedom to experiment. I truly believe marketing should be creative and sometimes even a little “crazy.”

I created seasonal beverage series inspired by social media trends, such as a sakura latte in April. I organized an Outdoor Movie Night as a pre-launch experiment to test customer acceptance of a new sparkling drink. I also created a space in the café for art students to sell their designs, giving them a small platform while creating an additional source of revenue for us.

From Concept to Project

My ideas often start with a simple observation from everyday life, or when I am struck by a story, an image, or a piece of music. What I enjoy most is the journey of turning those ideas into something real, even when they do not work perfectly the first time.

My Chrome extension, DontFeedMonster, came from a small frustration of my own. A normal to-do list did not make unfinished tasks feel real enough, and sometimes I could not even find my physical list on the table. So I designed Momo using Codex, a pink monster who loves sleeping and eating pizza. When you delay a task, Momo gets fed. Feed it too much, and it gradually becomes crazier and annoyes you.

Momo is one example of an idea that made its way from my head into the real world, and I still have plenty more waiting to come alive.

Sense of Humor & Reading the Room

My school experience includes nearly 10 years as a class monitor and 1.5 years as a co-founder of a campus café. I learned to read the room from an early age. As a class monitor, I had to “manage” my classmates on behalf of teachers while, technically, being one of the people being managed in the group. It taught me to understand different perspectives and adjust how I communicate.

Later, while running the café, this instinct became useful in the real world. I negotiated partnerships with student clubs to increase foot traffic and worked with overseas education agencies looking for venues to host presentations on campus. Through these experiences, I learned how to communicate with different groups, understand what each side wants, negotiate around those interests(sometimes use a little humor to make things easier), and eventually turn conversations into relationships.
